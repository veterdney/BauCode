# ÖKOBAUDAT anchors carbon; assumptions carry everything else

The 40-material dataset (M01–M40) is built and saved as one UTF-8 CSV with 40 rows and 172 columns. It holds every requested property, the 15 indicator values, their 1–5 ratings and a provenance tag for each value that did not come from ÖKOBAUDAT. The main finding is a layer split. Your ÖKOBAUDAT 2024-II export alone supports **7 robust, comparable indicators for all 40 materials**, and they decide the whole Environment criterion plus one Well-being indicator (density as thermal and acoustic mass). The other **8 indicators rest on assumptions or second-hand tables**: all of Cost, two of the three Durability indicators, and two of the three Well-being indicators. Even the "cited" inputs behind those 8 often come from paywalled standards that the researchers could not open (DIN 4108-4, EN ISO 10456, BKI, DIN 4109). So the ratings are only as strong as the layer they come from. Treat Environment as near-decision-grade, and treat Cost, Durability and most of Well-being as a structured hypothesis to verify. One time-sensitive caveat applies: **35 of the 40 chosen datasets (including all 33 Sphera generic ones) expire at the end of 2026**, and Germany's energy law changed this year. The thermal notes report that the GEG was replaced by the Gebäudemodernisierungsgesetz (GModG), in force since 29 July 2026, with the component U-value limits carried over unchanged.

## The 2024-II export covers all 40 materials, with expiry and proxy caveats

The export holds 4,308 datasets, and **4,300 of them conform to EN 15804+A2** ([ÖKOBAUDAT](https://www.oekobaudat.de/en)). Every material therefore draws on the same indicator set. Thirty-three materials map to German generic datasets (reference year 2023, valid to 2026). Six use association-average or representative datasets: brick, structural steel, CLT, OSB, plasterboard and window. Reinforced concrete (M02) has no dataset of its own. It is **derived as 0.98 kg C25/30 + 0.02 kg rebar**, i.e. about 47 kg steel/m³. Typical German reinforcement ratios of 100–150 kg/m³ would raise its GWP in proportion. Generic datasets carry built-in **10–30 % uncertainty surcharges** meant to be conservative ([ÖKOBAUDAT Manual](https://www.oekobaudat.de/fileadmin/downloads/2021-06-28_OEBD-Manual.pdf)). M01 concrete shows the effect: generic 0.096 kg CO₂e/kg against 0.075 for the German average dataset. Mixing generic data for some materials with specific EPDs for others would distort the ranking, and the dataset avoids that. EPD datasets are removed from ÖKOBAUDAT when they expire ([Manual](https://www.oekobaudat.de/fileadmin/downloads/2021-06-28_OEBD-Manual.pdf)), so the whole table should be re-checked against the next release.

Proxies apply in four places. Generic "mineral wool" is not split into stone and glass wool, so flat-roof wool (145 kg/m³) stands in for M20 and pitched-roof wool (30 kg/m³) for M21. M25 hemp fleece shows an anomalous **+1.53 kg CO₂e/kg A1-A3** despite 0.42 kg biogenic C/kg, and the CSV does not explain why. M38 is a full ETICS adhesive-plus-render system, not render alone. M40 uses a generic rammed-earth wall. Toxicity is the clearest data gap. HTP-c, HTP-nc and PM are populated for **only 2 of the 40 chosen datasets** (M16, M37), because generic datasets never report them. Under EN 15804+A2 these are optional indicators ([One Click LCA](https://oneclicklca.com/en/resources/articles/en-15804-changes-epds)), and several A2 indicators carry a "use with care" disclaimer ([EPD International](https://environdec.com/pcr/env-perf-indic/gpi5/indic-env-impact)). The dataset therefore keeps toxicity as a descriptive property, with substances of concern and AVV hazardous-waste codes, rather than as a rated indicator.

End-of-life process type comes from two layers. The CSV-inferred route (`obd_eol_process_type_inferred`) is read from the C3/C4/D modules and the MFR/MER flows. It sorts the materials into mineral crushing, landfill only, incineration with energy recovery, and metal recycling. Module D for steel is a **burden** (M09 +0.21, M10 +0.14 kg CO₂e/kg) because EAF production consumes more scrap than it returns ([ÖKOBAUDAT dataset 20c29cbd](https://www.oekobaudat.de/OEKOBAU.DAT/resource/processes/20c29cbd-f9a2-4518-a1ce-c681246c29c8)). That is why D is reported separately and never netted into a score. The second layer is an assumed practice-oriented EoL type with AVV code and circularity score, tagged ASSUMED.

## Seven CSV indicators and eight assumption-based indicators make up the 15

The framework assigns each criterion its indicators by data source. Environment draws entirely on the CSV. Well-being gets one CSV indicator: density, which drives thermal storage and airborne sound insulation through the mass law. Cost and Durability get no CSV indicator at all. The 1–5 scale runs from 1 (worst) to 5 (best). Where the notes give a score as a range (for example "4–5"), the dataset uses the lower bound. This follows DGNB's rule that the weakest single aspect sets the level ([DGNB ENV1.2](https://dgnb.de/filestorages/Downloads_unprotected/dokumente/kriterien/dgnb-kriterium-env-1-2-gebaeude-neubau-version-2023.pdf)).

The Environment thresholds are author-set bands on a roughly logarithmic scale. They are not taken from any tool. ENV1 and ENV2 appear as a pair on purpose. A1-A3 alone credits the stored biogenic carbon (KVH −1.19 kg CO₂e/kg), while A1-A3+C3+C4 cancels the −1/+1 accounting (KVH +0.44). The notes argue this second figure is the fairest single comparison between bio-based and mineral materials. Cost bands follow the service-life notes. They copy DGNB's benchmark interpolation, where the GWP indicator uses anchors at 0.5×, 0.75×, 1× and 2.25× a reference value ([DGNB ENV1.1](https://www.dgnb.de/filestorages/Downloads_unprotected/dokumente/kriterien/dgnb-kriterium-env-1-1-gebaeude-neubau-version-2023.pdf)). They also use BRE's principle that ratings are "only relevant within a specific element group" ([BRE Green Guide](https://tools.bregroup.com/greenguide/page.jsp?id=2088)). Accordingly, cost is rated only within unit-consistent peer groups (concrete per m³, masonry per m² wall, insulation per m² at U ≈ 0.20, and so on). Three singletons stay unrated: aluminium, bitumen and windows. Placing A5 site waste under Cost is a judgement call. It acts as a purchase-and-disposal surcharge, and Environment was kept CSV-only. Move it if your criteria definitions differ.

One structural weakness should be stated plainly. **Per-kg environmental ratings penalise lightweight insulation**: EPS scores ENV2 = 1 at 7.17 kg CO₂e/kg, yet a building uses only about 1.5–2 kg of it per m² floor area. The dataset includes the assumed kg/m² BGF so you can rescale. Do that rescaling before ranking insulation against structure.

## Thermal, fire and acoustic values rest heavily on paywalled standards

λ is cited for only **15 of 40 materials**, mostly from the SEAI DEAP compilation of EN ISO 10456 and CIBSE values ([SEAI](https://deapguide.seai.ie/fabric/heat-loss-u-value/thermal-conductivity)). The other 25 are TYPICAL manufacturer ranges or UNVERIFIED-STD values that the researcher recalled from DIN 4108-4 and EN ISO 10456. Neither standard could be opened, because both are sold by DIN Media ([DIN Media](https://www.dinmedia.de/de/norm/din-4108-4/327502903)). μ is weaker still: **28 of 40 are UNVERIFIED-STD**. The cited exceptions are gypsum (4/10) and cork (5/10) ([Wikipedia DE](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasserdampfdiffusionswiderstandszahl)), plus mineral wool (μ = 1) and foam glass (practically vapour-tight) ([baunetzwissen](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/drucken/stoffeigenschaften-und-wasserdampfdiffusionswiderstand-4383625)). Every specific heat value is unverified. The dataset stores λ as a min/max pair plus a note, because modern products often sit outside the normative tables. Perforated brick, for example, ranges from 0.07 W/(m·K) (filled, with DIBt approval) to 0.77 (dense brickwork).

**The legal frame for U-value targets has changed.** According to the thermal notes, the cabinet adopted the GModG on 13 May 2026. It was promulgated on 28 July 2026 and its main provisions apply from 29 July 2026 ([Bundesregierung](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/neues-gebaeudemodernisierungsgesetz-2430284)). It implements EPBD 2024/1275 and makes zero-emission buildings mandatory from 2028 for new public buildings and from 2030 for all new buildings ([Görg](https://www.goerg.de/de/aktuelles/veroeffentlichungen/17-07-2026/gebaeudemodernisierungsgesetz-gmodg-schafft-geg-ab-was-sich-aendert-und-was-bleibt); [Solarserver](https://www.solarserver.de/2026/07/10/gebaeudemodernisierungsgesetz-bundestag-beschliesst-reform/)). Revised reference buildings, primary-energy factors and phased life-cycle GHG reporting (§ 88b) follow from 2027 ([baunetzwissen GModG](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/drucken/gebaeudemodernisierungsgesetz-gmodg-10441370)). Residential buildings face no comparable renovation obligation ([GvW](https://www.gvw.com/aktuelles/blog/detail/gmodg-in-kraft-was-nun-fuer-bestand-und-neubau-gilt)). The renovation limits in Anlage 7 read the same in the old GEG text and in the consolidated GModG text: **wall 0.24, pitched roof 0.24, flat roof 0.20, ground/basement 0.30, window Uw 1.3 W/(m²K)** ([gesetze-im-internet Anlage 7](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/geg/anlage_7.html); [buzer.de Anlage 7 GModG](https://www.buzer.de/Anlage_7_GModG.htm)). The new-build reference values are wall 0.28, roof 0.20, ground 0.35 and window 1.3 ([Anlage 1](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/geg/anlage_1.html)). The dataset attaches these to each material through an ASSUMED typical-element mapping. It adds the EH55/EH40 guideline values (wall 0.20/0.16, roof 0.14/0.11, window 0.9/0.7) from a 2021-era manufacturer summary ([Wienerberger](https://www.wienerberger.de/content/dam/wienerberger/germany/marketing/documents-magazines/instructions-guidelines/wall/DE_MKT_DOC_PON_Schnelluebersicht_GEG_KfW_Wienerberger.pdf)). These are guideline values, not KfW requirements. The Passive House values (opaque elements 0.10–0.15, window ≤0.80) are UNVERIFIED because passiv.de could not be reached. Two points remain open: whether Anlage 1 changes on 1 January 2027, and what the current BEG component values are.

Fire classification splits the same way. The DIN 4102 class is cited for 22 materials from a mapping source ([Forum Verlag](https://forum-verlag.com/blog-bs/brandschutzklassen)), but the EN 13501-1 class is cited for only 3. Mineral materials are A1 and gypsum boards A2. Timber is typically D-s2,d0 (B2) and polymer foams E. Ten entries carry a "product-dependent" flag, including PIR, cellulose, wood fibre, the floorings and windows. Fire **requirements** are better sourced than the classes. Load-bearing elements need REI 30 in building classes 2–3, REI 60 in class 4 and REI 90 in class 5 ([Sto](https://www.sto.de/cepcom/de/Dokumente/Bewusst-bauen/uebersicht_gebaeudeklassen_gemaess_mbo-(1).pdf); [BauNetz](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/drucken/anforderungen-an-bauteile-3138963)). Since MHolzBauRL 2024, timber is allowed up to "abweichend feuerbeständig" in compartments of ≤400 m² ([BauNetz MHolzBauRL](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/drucken/musterholzbaurichtlinie-mholzbaurl-2024-6970262)). ETICS must be B2 up to 7 m, B1 from 7 to 22 m and non-combustible above 22 m ([BauNetz WDVS](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/drucken/brandschutz-bei-waermedaemmverbundsystemen-845354)). EPS systems need stone-wool fire barriers of ≥200 mm with a melting point of ≥1000 °C ([DIBt 2016](https://www.zapf-daigfuss.de/sites/default/files/downloads/2016/10_alternative_brandschutzmassnahme_eps-wdvs.pdf)). For sound, the multi-family minimums in DIN 4109-1:2018 are **party wall R'w ≥ 53 dB and slab R'w ≥ 54 / L'n,w ≤ 50 dB**. The increased levels in DIN 4109-5 are 56, 57 and 45 dB ([Schöck](https://www.schoeck.com/view/14142)). Per-material acoustic data are thin. Dynamic stiffness is cited only for the five resilient layers ([BauNetz](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/daemmstoffe/fachwissen/eigenschaften/dynamische-steifigkeit-152176)). The mass-law Rw values (24 cm calcium silicate ≈ 60 dB, 100 mm CLT ≈ 32–35 dB) are UNVERIFIED. Fire and sound are therefore stored as constraints and context, not as scored indicators. They work as pass/fail filters for a given building class, and averaging them into a score would hide a non-compliance.

## Cost, durability and well-being inputs are assumptions around a few cited anchors

Service lives come from the BBSR/BNB 2017 table, which was read through an automated summariser, so its codes need checking against the PDF ([BNB Nutzungsdauern 2017](https://www.nachhaltigesbauen.de/fileadmin/pdf/Nutzungsdauer_Bauteile/BNB_Nutzungsdauern_von_Bauteilen_2017-02-24.pdf)). The table puts nearly every structural, masonry, plaster and stone element at **≥50 years**, ETICS at 40, bitumen and glazing units at 30, and **linoleum and PVC at 20** (two replacements in 50 years). Seven mappings are assumed, including OSB, rebar, straw, hemp and cellulose. Service life depends on where the material is used: stone wool scores 40 years in ETICS and ≥50 inside a construction. The technical lives from Baunetz/IfB (80 years for massive elements) separate a 5 from a 4 ([Baunetz Wissen](https://www.baunetzwissen.de/drucken/nutzungsdauer-725797)).

Costs are the weakest layer. BKI unit prices are paywalled ([BKI Flyer 2025](https://www.aksachsen.org/fileadmin/Image_Archive/Berufspolitik/BKI/BKI_Flyer_2025.pdf)). Only the insulation costs are cited, as early-2026 installed €/m² at U ≈ 0.20, with VAT status unstated: EPS 49–72, wood fibre 80–115, foam glass 105–165 ([reduco.ai](https://reduco.ai/blog/daemmung/daemmstoffe-vergleich-uebersicht)). The other 31 materials carry ASSUMED net 2026 ranges (±30 %). Index them with the Destatis residential construction price index, which rose **+5.5 % year on year in August 2026** (timber construction +7.2 %, roofing +7.6 %) ([Destatis](https://www.destatis.de/DE/Presse/Pressemitteilungen/2026/10/PD26_360_61261.html)). Geometry per m² floor area is entirely ASSUMED. It is based on a 4-storey reference multi-family house with external wall 0.55, roof 0.27 and windows 0.18 m²/m² BGF. Reinforced concrete dominates at roughly 600–800 kg/m² BGF.

The Well-being and toxicity layer has cited frameworks but assumed material ratings. The February 2026 AgBB scheme sets **TVOC ≤1.0 mg/m³ at 28 days**, SVOC ≤0.1 mg/m³ and R ≤1, with a formaldehyde NIK of 100 µg/m³ ([UBA/AgBB 2026](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/system/files/document/AgBB-Bewertungsschema%202026_Bundesgesundheitsblatt.pdf)). DGNB ENV1.2 scores substance avoidance in four quality levels, QS1 to QS4 ([DGNB ENV1.2](https://dgnb.de/filestorages/Downloads_unprotected/dokumente/kriterien/dgnb-kriterium-env-1-2-gebaeude-neubau-version-2023.pdf)). No material-specific chamber data were retrieved, however, so every IAQ rating is ASSUMED. Examples: PVC flooring M–H, OSB and PUR M, mineral materials L. Moisture buffering is cited only for **clay plaster (1.67 g/(m²·%RH)) versus NHL lime plaster (0.95)** ([Cascione et al.](https://core.ac.uk/download/189163604.pdf)). A5 waste rates are assumed defaults, broadly consistent with the classic finding that 1–10 % of purchased material leaves the site as waste ([Bossink & Brouwers](https://ascelibrary.com/doi/10.1061/%28ASCE%290733-9364%281996%29122%3A1%2855%29)). The real-world end-of-life context is cited. Germany recycled **81.7 % of construction rubble in 2022**, but no high-quality gypsum recycling share was reported for its 0.64 Mt of gypsum waste (59.5 % went to mining backfill or landfill construction, 40.5 % to landfill) ([UBA](https://www.umweltbundesamt.de/daten/ressourcen-abfall/verwertung-entsorgung-ausgewaehlter-abfallarten/bauabfaelle)).

## Ratings reward timber and earth and penalise foams, with the confidence gaps above

On Environment, the CSV-driven means rank **CLT (4.67), KVH and cellulose (4.33) and OSB (4.17)** highest. **EPS, XPS, PIR and foam glass (1.5)** rank lowest, and aluminium, PVC flooring and windows score 2.0. Biogenic carbon and renewable energy shares drive the top of that order. The bottom is driven by fossil feedstock and, for foam glass, by landfill-only end of life. Durability inverts part of the picture. Resilient floors (PVC 1.5, linoleum 1.67) and bitumen (1.5) score lowest because of BNB replacement cycles. Natural stone (4.67), the metals (4.5) and concrete (4.33) score highest. Well-being favours rammed earth (5.0) and the bio-based insulants (4.5), while PVC sits at 2.0. Conflicts within a criterion are informative. Straw scores 4 on carbon and 1 on moisture resistance, and plasterboard scores well on IAQ but 1.33 on cost under the assumed prices. Each criterion mean needs at least two rated indicators. The CSV reports how many CSV-driven and how many assumption-based ratings stand behind every row, so you can filter by confidence.

## Conclusion

The two layers carry different confidence. Environment is grounded in data. Its main risks are procedural: the generic datasets expire in 2026, the M02 composite and the hemp and mineral-wool proxies need review, and per-kg ratings must be converted to functional units before comparing across element groups. Everything else is a scaffold. The weights a user later applies to Cost or Durability will magnify assumptions, not evidence. The cheapest way to strengthen the framework is to buy or license four sources, in this order: BKI unit prices, which turn all of Cost from assumed to cited; DIN 4108-4 and EN ISO 10456, for λ, μ and c; the BNB PDF itself; and AgBB or eco-INSTITUT test data for the room-facing materials. Fire and sound should stay as building-class filters rather than scores. The GModG has kept the envelope U-values but adds life-cycle GHG reporting from 2027. That makes the CSV-driven carbon indicators the part of this framework most likely to become regulatory input, so re-run the build script against each new ÖKOBAUDAT release.
